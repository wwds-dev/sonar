---

  - `sonar/` — "SONAR" PySide6 paper-trading terminal, **eight tabs**: Portfolio (the landing
    page since 2026-10-08 — the user's paper book as a picture: P&L now, account value,
    invested split into cash spent on longs and stock borrowed for shorts, the cost of every
    stop hitting, the account's value over time, every position as a risk-sized tile and as
    a card; `Portfolio.log_equity` writes the account-value log hourly and
    `seed_equity_log` rebuilds the days before it existed from the book's own records and
    real closes), Assets (a 26-instrument screener), Wire (24 newswires across nine press
    blocs, the earnings/IPO calendar, and alerts), Book (paper positions and the calibration
    table), Macro (regime plus central-bank communication), Lab (replay the model over real
    bars, attribute the score component by component, or step through history making your
    own calls — and, at its foot, the hourly BTC up/down model priced against Polymarket,
    the only place an independent direction is asserted; it was the landing page, "Terminal",
    until 2026-10-08, and `_terminal_tab()` still builds it, hosted inside the Lab page),
    Playmaker (sports prop pricing across **seven sports** — NFL, College Football,
    NBA, MMA (UFC), International Football, Golf, Cycling — in `sonar/playmaker/`, the
    only home for that logic; the standalone scaffold that reserved the name was removed.
    **Football means international team games only**; a test asserts no club code can
    creep back in. The *pricing* half is sport-agnostic — `devig.py` and `staking.py` are
    odds arithmetic and never learn what they are pricing, which is why a three-way market
    needed no special casing. The *rating* half is not, and `Sport.shape` says so:
    `scores` (two sides and a scoreline -> Elo, plus Dixon-Coles for football), `winners`
    (two sides, no scoreline -> MMA, plain Elo), `field` (a finishing order -> **no model
    at all**, because a golf tournament is not a head-to-head contest and pretending
    otherwise would invent an answer). Cycling additionally has **no results feed** —
    ESPN serves no cycling endpoint and the free alternatives are unofficial scrapers —
    so `espn_paths` is empty, which is a real state and not an oversight. The tab states
    per sport what is available.) **Playmaker holds
    the same line as the rest of the app as of Sep 2026**: a language model's percentage
    cannot size a bet (`staking.Estimate` carries a source with every probability and
    returns a zero stake for a narrative one), both sides of a market are required because
    a margin is how far prices sum past certainty, and the feature with a published track
    record is the cross-book screen — Kaunitz et al. (2017) — which finds where books
    disagree with *each other* rather than predicting anything. It now also *predicts*:
    Elo in FiveThirtyEight's published form (`ratings.py`), Dixon-Coles for football
    (`poisson.py`), Pythagorean as a cross-check, fed by one keyless ESPN adapter
    (`results.py`). **Nothing predicts until it has been measured** — `scoring.py` is
    walk-forward and `calibrated_estimate()` is the only route from a model to a stake,
    refusing for anything unmeasured or beaten by the base rate, exactly as
    `calibration.py` does on the markets side. NFL/NBA/EPL all came back KEEP (skill
    +0.071/+0.120/+0.139); Dixon-Coles scores RPS 0.2149 on held-out EPL matches.
    `sonar/playmaker/MODELS.md` surveys the literature and carries the staged plan; read
    it before touching the scoring. Four traps in `results.py`, all measured rather than
    guessed: it sends **no User-Agent** (ESPN 403s a custom or browser-shaped one and
    accepts urllib's default — 3/3 vs 0/3; pasting in a Chrome string is impersonation
    *and* is the thing being blocked); a truncated chunked response raises
    `http.client.IncompleteRead`, an `HTTPException` and **not** an `OSError`, so it slips
    past the obvious except clause and silently costs a season; `limit=400` returns 400
    and `limit=1000` returns 1000 but **`limit=2000` returns twenty-five**, so the cap
    fails by quietly giving up rather than erroring; and arbitrary `from-to` date ranges
    are unreliable (identical requests that worked earlier in a session later returned
    400) while `dates=YYYY` and `dates=YYYYMM` have never failed — hence fetching a year
    whole and dropping to months only when a response comes back holding exactly the
    limit, which is the only signal ESPN gives that it truncated. The multi-market
    Polymarket board was **removed** in Aug 2026: it mirrored the crowd's own prices with no
    model behind them, and dropping it saved ~52MB/hour. As of the Sep 2026 reorg
    `sonar/sonar/macro/` and `sonar/sonar/playmaker/` are each their own git repo.
    **The GUI follows the Plain Language direction as of 2026-09-22** (README
    §"The Plain Language direction"). Three rules, all testable: proportional
    type for words and `theme.figure` — the same face with tabular numerals —
    for figures, with `theme.code` reserved for the two places that take a
    pasted table; `MUTED` and `FAINT` clear 4.5:1 against the panel they sit on;
    and the board is written in English, with every heading that names something
    non-obvious linking into the manual. Two traps it left behind. Every tab now
    shows **two names** — the plain one over the one the docs use — painted by
    `ui/tabs.py`, because Qt draws a `\n` in a tab label on one line and clips
    it; so `addTab` is `PlainTabs.add(widget, plain, was)` and the docs test
    greps for that call. And raising the background/panel contrast exposed that
    every `QLabel` inherits the blanket `QWidget { background }` rule and paints
    it over whatever panel it sits on — invisible while the two colours were
    three points apart on the same near-black, a dark box behind every cell once
    they were not. Labels and layout holders opt out in the stylesheet; a new
    widget that paints a rectangle it should not is this, again.

    **Two vocabularies, one layout.** `ui/words.py` holds a plain/expert mode
    (remembered in `wording.json` beside the other user data) and a toolbar
    button switches it. Plain is the default: words for headings, a second line
    under each figure, one plain sentence per row. Expert restores MOM/VOL/R:R/
    CONF, the ticker, single-line tabs, and drops the second lines. The rule it
    must keep is that it changes the **vocabulary and the density, never the
    layout** — `ASSET_COLS` carries `(plain, expert)` per column and one width,
    so the header re-captions in place and there is never a second board to keep
    correct. Anything that would need a different column set in one mode is the
    wrong feature; put it in both or in neither.

    The eighth tab is **Learn**: `static/docs.html` — the manual and its glossary —
    rendered inside the window by `ui/learn.py`, which strips the page's own CSS
    (variables, flexbox, gradients: Qt renders none of it, and silently keeps the
    rules it *can* parse, which is worse than none) and injects `<a name>` anchors,
    because Qt does not follow the `id` a browser follows. The HTML stays the single
    source — `tests/test_docs.py` checks it, and a second copy of the prose would be
    a second thing to keep true. Adding a tab means adding its row to that page's
    tabs table, which a test enforces.

    Two things to know before changing it. **It asserts no direction outside the Terminal
    tab, deliberately** — five pre-registered studies over 116,563 asset-days found no edge,
    the confidence score is a *notability* heuristic, and `P(profit)` stays pinned at its
    driftless `1/(1+R:R)` baseline until `calibration.py` measures drift from positions that
    actually closed. Do not add a feature that implies a direction without evidence; the Lab
    tab's component attribution is how you check whether a change earns its weight.
    **Two SONARs, one book (2026-10-08):** the engine lock still lets one process drive, but
    the other now *follows* it — the daemon writes its URL into `engine.lock`, the window
    mirrors it over localhost (`Live._wait_for_lock`, `/api/book`, `/api/wire`) and forwards
    buy/short/close/knobs/protocol/LLM read (`/api/trade`, `/api/close`, `/api/config`,
    `/api/read`); whoever waits takes the lock over the moment it is free. A second window
    has no URL to follow and just waits. Before this the window beside the agent showed an
    empty book and the agent started beside a window never drove. **They only meet in one
    directory**: a frozen app's state is `~/Library/Application Support/SONAR`, a checkout's
    is its `data/`, and the agent runs from the checkout — so for weeks it ran a second
    experiment beside the installed app's. `SONAR_DATA` overrides the directory
    (`sonar/paths.py`) and `install_agent.sh` sets it in the plist to the app's; the old
    `data/` run is archived in place and is what a source run without the variable gets. And
    **there is no live trading venue, by design** — `execution.py` is a full order guard
    (caps, confirmation, idempotency, flatten, audit log) whose only implementation is a
    simulator, and `alpaca.py` makes the live endpoint unreachable. `GOING_LIVE.md` is the
    guide for wiring one; `venues.py` records where each watchlist row could actually be
    traded — on the 129-row watchlist, 58 rows only via a proxy and one
    (Monero) not at all; run `python -m sonar.venues` for the live census.

    Two Qt traps that have each shipped once. `shutdown()` must name **every** QThread the
    window owns — Qt aborts the process when a running thread is destroyed, and a thread
    added without being listed is a SIGABRT on quit. And a `QLabel` that does not wrap
    reports a sizeHint as wide as its text, so one long prose label sets the minimum width
    of its tab and therefore the whole window; `label(..., wrap=True)` and
    `tests/test_layout.py` guard it.

    **The close button hides to the menu bar; only Quit quits** — the same shape as Lab
    Hub, and settled by the user on 2026-09-16 after a day of it briefly doing the
    opposite. The engine keeps settling hours with the window gone, which is the data the
    app exists to collect; `main.py --headless` and the launchd agent in
    `scripts/install_agent.sh` are the no-window forms of the same thing. Cmd-Q and the
    menu bar's Quit are the real exits, and both must stay real: macOS implements a quit
    by sending a close event to every window, so the hide-on-close guard has to be
    released for a genuine quit or Cmd-Q silently does nothing (`eventFilter`, on
    `QEvent.Quit`). `tests/test_window_close.py` pins down both halves.

    **"SONAR won't close" was reported five times and was never about the close button.**
    Four of the five screenshots showed a blank **white** window — and the app is themed
    `#080b11`, so white is a window Qt never painted, i.e. a dead event loop, not a
    refused close. Three of the four had separate causes (a full-screen Space left behind,
    the Dock reopening the window its own hide had just closed, the UI thread blocked in a
    fetch). The fourth, found 2026-09-19, was `shutdown()` calling **`QThread.terminate()`**
    on a thread that would not stop. That is the same trap this file already describes in
    the test suite, shipped in the app: terminate kills the thread wherever it stands, and
    a thread running Python holds the **GIL**, which is then never released — every Python
    thread blocks in `take_gil` forever, the event loop included. It fired routinely, not
    rarely: `live.stop()` only lands between fetches, so any quit during an in-flight
    request had to outlast an 8–30s socket timeout. The fix is to stop trying to stop the
    thread and end the process instead (`os._exit`), which skips the QThread destructors
    whose `qFatal()` was the only reason terminate was ever wanted; nothing is lost,
    because the engine writes through on every change and the engine lock is a PID file
    the next launch reclaims. **Never call `QThread.terminate()` here** —
    `tests/test_shutdown.py` fails the build on it by AST, and a companion test quits a
    real subprocess mid-fetch, where the old code hangs rather than failing.

    The fifth, reported 2026-09-22, was the freeze again, through a door the rule did not
    cover: `_rescan()` called `institutions.payload()` **inside `self.lock`**, and the UI
    thread takes that same lock every second in `MainWindow.refresh()`. The payload fetches
    four RSS feeds at a 12s timeout when its fifteen-minute cache expires, so every fifteen
    minutes the window blocked on a mutex for the length of a fetch — blank, ignoring the
    close button, alive again a few seconds later. Nothing fetched on the UI thread, which
    is why the existing rule and its tests said the code was clean. **Waiting on a lock held
    across a fetch is the same freeze**: compute first, take the lock for the assignment
    only. `tests/test_ui_thread.py` now checks that by behaviour and by AST, over both
    `sonar/core.py` and `ui/app.py`. Two smaller things came out of chasing it: a hide
    deferred by a full-screen close must not land on a window the user has since reopened
    (so every reveal goes through `MainWindow.reveal()`, which cancels it), and Qt gives no
    signal for the *end* of a Space transition — `WindowStateChange` and the restored
    geometry both arrive about 1ms after `showNormal()`, with the animation still to come,
    so `FULLSCREEN_EXIT_MS` has to stay a timer.

    **Nothing on the UI thread may fetch — nor wait on anything that does.**
    `_refresh_wire()` used to call
    `news.headlines()` and `events.payload()`, both of which fetch when their cache ages
    out — news every eight minutes, twenty-four GETs at a ten-second timeout. Whichever
    thread reached the expired cache first did the work, so the window could freeze for
    half a minute: a blank white rectangle ignoring the close button, which a user
    reported. Use `news.cached()` / `events.cached_payload()`, which cannot fetch; the
    poll thread's `_rescan()` warms both. `tests/test_ui_thread.py` and a case in
    `test_refresh.py` hold the line.

    `static/docs.html` is the in-app manual and **§1 is a plain-English primer** — the app
    is aimed at someone with no finance background, and every tooltip and doc section is
    written to that. `tests/test_docs.py` enforces the parts that rot silently: the table
    of contents matches the sections, numbering is gapless, every `§N` cross-reference and
    `#anchor` resolves, every tab the window builds has a row in the tabs table, every
    registered sport is named using its own label, and the glossary still defines its
    twenty-five terms. Renumbering sections is exactly the edit that breaks a `§6` three
    paragraphs down, so change docs with the test running.

    A third trap, in the tests rather than the app, and it cost hours before it was found.
    Three tests build a real `MainWindow`, whose constructor **starts the poll thread
    immediately**; each then called `win.poll.live.stop()`, which stops the *next* fetch
    while the first is already away. If that fetch is still running at teardown,
    `shutdown()` waits four seconds and then calls `QThread.terminate()` — and terminating
    a thread that holds the **GIL** never releases it, so the main thread blocks in
    `take_gil` forever. The suite passed or hung on whether the network happened to answer
    in time; one run sat wedged for 5h50m at 0.9% CPU and was misread as two pytest runs
    contending for the engine lock. `tests/conftest.py` closes it off for the whole
    session: no sockets, `paths.user_data_base` pointed at `tmp_path` (only two tests did
    that for themselves, so the rest were taking the engine lock in the real application
    directory while the user's own app might be running), and `Live.warmup`/`Live._poll`
    neutralised so the loop spins without work. Do not "fix" a hanging Qt test by raising
    the wait — find what the thread is blocked on.

    **Those guards must be session-scoped, and were not.** They were `autouse=True` at
    *function* scope, and pytest builds fixtures highest-scope-first — so for the three
    tests they were written to protect, all of which take a **module**-scoped `window`
    fixture, not one of them was in force when the window was built. Those tests were
    still starting the real `Live.run()`, still taking the engine lock in the user's real
    application directory, and still going to the network; the poll thread then never
    finished, so the fixture's own teardown reached the terminate above. Fixed 2026-09-19
    by installing the same patches from a session-scoped fixture, with the function-scoped
    ones kept for per-test `tmp_path` and for naming the test in the network error. The
    suite went from ~9s-and-wedging-1-run-in-3 to 9s and deterministic. **A guard that
    protects a module-scoped fixture has to outrank it** — check the scope, not just the
    `autouse`.
