SONAR — how it works

A market scanner and paper-trading terminal that tries very hard not to overstate what it knows. Live prices, live news, a probability model, and five studies that failed to find an edge — all reported plainly.

The one thing to understand first. SONAR reads real market data and real news, but every position is paper. There is no funded account, no wallet, and no route to real money anywhere in the code. It exists to show, transparently, how a model and a set of heuristics actually perform — which, so far, is no better than chance.

1Start here — plain English

You do not need a finance background to use this. This section explains what the app is for, the one idea worth holding onto, and every piece of jargon you will meet in it. Everything after this assumes you have read it.

What SONAR actually does

It watches the price and the news flow of 26 things you could invest in — shares, indices, currencies, commodities, crypto — and flags the ones where something unusual is happening. You can then take a position with pretend money and watch, over weeks, whether those flags were worth anything. There is no real account anywhere in it.

The point is not to be told what to buy. The point is to find out whether a thing that looks like a signal actually pays — which is a question almost nobody asks honestly, including most people selling you signals.

The one idea to hold onto. A high confidence score means “something is going on here, worth a look”. It does not mean “this will go up”. SONAR deliberately refuses to tell you which way to bet, because five separate studies across 116,563 asset-days failed to find any reliable way to tell. Anything that claims otherwise, here or elsewhere, is guessing.

Words you will meet

Roughly in the order you will bump into them.

WordWhat it means
PositionA bet you currently hold on something. You open it and later close it; the difference is your profit or loss.
Long / shortLong = you own it and profit if it rises. Short = the reverse, you profit if it falls.
Paper tradingDoing all of that with imaginary money, to test an idea without risking anything. Everything in SONAR is paper.
VolatilityHow much a price jumps around, day to day. High volatility means bigger swings in both directions — it says nothing about which way.
MomentumHow much something has already moved recently. A description of the past. Whether it predicts the future is exactly what §10 tested, and the answer was no.
Stop / targetTwo prices you decide in advance: the stop is where you give up and take the loss, the target is where you take the profit. Setting both before you enter is the single most useful discipline in this whole app.
R:RReward divided by risk. If your target is 15% away and your stop is 10% away, that is 1.5. A bigger number sounds better and is not free — see §5.
Mark to marketWhat a position is worth right now if you closed it this second, as opposed to what you paid.
DrawdownHow far your money has fallen from its best point. The number that actually makes people quit.
HorizonHow far ahead you are looking — a day, a week, a month. It changes which numbers SONAR shows you, because a signal that matters over a month is noise over a day.
Basis pointOne hundredth of a percent. “50 bps” = 0.5%. Used because “half a percent of a percent” gets confusing fast.

Words about probability

WordWhat it means
Base rateHow often a thing happens in general, before you know anything specific. If home teams win 54% of the time, that is the base rate, and any model has to beat it to be worth having.
Expected valueWhat you would win or lose on average per attempt if you repeated a bet forever. Positive is worth taking, negative is not, however good any single outcome looks.
CalibrationWhether the numbers mean what they say: of all the things called 70% likely, did about 70% actually happen? A model can be confident and useless; calibration is the check that catches it.
Out-of-sampleTesting a model on data it has never seen. The opposite — scoring it on the data it learned from — makes anything look brilliant and is the most common way people fool themselves.
BacktestRunning a strategy over history to see what it would have done. Useful, and easy to abuse: see §10 for five that were run properly and found nothing.
Brier scoreA mark out of one for a set of probability guesses, where lower is better. 0 is perfect foresight, 0.25 is a coin flip on everything.

Words in the Playmaker tab

WordWhat it means
OddsThe price of a bet. −150 means stake 150 to win 100; +130 means stake 100 to win 130. The bigger the number after a plus, the less likely the bookmaker thinks it is.
Implied probabilityThe chance a price is quietly claiming. −150 implies 60%. Turn every price into one of these and you can compare a bookmaker's opinion with your own.
The vigAlso margin, juice or overround — the bookmaker's cut. Add up what both sides of a market imply and you get more than 100%; the excess is theirs. Usually about 4.8%. It is why betting at random loses money even when you win half the time.
DeviggingTaking that cut back out to see what the bookmaker really thinks. Sounds like a detail; it is the difference between a real edge and an imaginary one, and how you do it changes the answer — see §13.
KellyA formula for how much of your money to put on a bet so it grows fastest over many bets without going broke. Famously aggressive, so Playmaker uses a fraction of it and sizes on the pessimistic end of what it believes.
Closing lineThe final price before an event starts, at a serious bookmaker. It is the most accurate probability that exists for a sporting event, and beating it consistently is the only real proof anyone knows what they are doing.

Words in the Macro tab

Macro means the whole economy, as opposed to any one company. These six numbers are the weather everything else happens in.

WordWhat it means
YieldWhat a government pays to borrow. The 10-year yield is the benchmark nearly everything else is priced against — mortgages, company debt, and what a share is worth today.
Yield curveThe 10-year rate minus the 2-year rate. Normally positive, because lending for longer should pay more. When it goes negative — “inverted” — lenders are betting rates will be cut, which usually means they expect a downturn. It has preceded most US recessions, often by a year or more.
Fed fundsThe interest rate the US central bank sets — the lever it pulls to cool inflation (raise) or support growth (cut). Every other rate takes its cue from it.
VIXHow much movement traders are paying up for over the next month. Under 15 is calm, over 25 is nervous, over 35 is a crisis. Like volatility, it says how big — never which way.
CPIHow much consumer prices rose over a year. Central banks usually target 2%. Well above and rates tend to rise; well below and they tend to fall.
Real yieldThe 10-year rate after subtracting inflation — what a lender actually earns in purchasing power. Negative means money sitting in government debt is losing value, which pushes it toward shares and gold.

The provider layer

Sources sit behind one interface with a capability (quotes / bars / FX / crypto), a tier (keyless or keyed) and an on-off switch. Requests walk the enabled providers in order and take the first that answers, so a vendor going down is a skipped provider rather than a broken app.

One real fragility, found by building this. Switch Yahoo off and crypto still resolves through CoinGecko and FX through Frankfurter — but equities return nothing. They have no keyless second source, so most of the watchlist depends on a single undocumented endpoint. A free Finnhub key closes that gap.

4The hourly model

Each hour Polymarket asks whether the BTC/USDT candle will close at or above its open. Part-way through, the open o and current price c are known; the rest is modelled as a driftless random walk with per-hour volatility σ and remaining fraction τ:

P(up) = Φ( ln(c / o) / (σ · √τ) )
  • At the top of the hour (τ=1, c=o) it returns exactly 0.5 — no information, no edge.
  • As the hour runs out it collapses toward 1 or 0 on the current sign.

The only disagreement with the market comes from the volatility estimate: SONAR uses realised vol while the market prices its own implied vol. That is a real but thin edge, and frequently negative after the spread.

5The three numbers

Every Assets row carries three, and they answer different questions.

NumberWhat it means
CONF Notability, 0–100: news 0.35, momentum 0.30, catalyst 0.20, volatility 0.15. It ranks what is worth a look. It is not the odds of profit.
R:R Reward divided by risk, from a volatility-scaled target and stop. 1.5 means the target sits 1.5× as far away as the stop.
P(PROF) The probability of touching the target before the stop.
R:R and P(profit) are the same number twice. For a driftless walk the barrier maths fixes the hit rate at stop/(target+stop), so P(profit) = 1/(1+R:R) and EV = P·target − (1−P)·stop = 0 exactly. A fatter target buys a proportionally lower hit rate. No arrangement of barriers creates profit — only drift does, and drift has to be measured before it is used.

That is why P(PROF) reads a flat 40% across the board and is drawn in grey: with R:R fixed at 1.5 and no measured edge, that is the honest number. It moves only when calibration finds real drift.

There is deliberately no Bullish/Bearish lean. There used to be; the research below found the momentum behind it was worth nothing, so it was deleted rather than tuned. The NEWS column shows how unusual today's coverage is — Quiet / Normal / Elevated / Spike — which is a notability flag and not a direction.

6The paper book

buy and short open a paper position on any row, sized so that being stopped out costs a fixed slice of the book. Two very different instruments therefore risk the same cash at their stops, which is the only sizing rule that treats them comparably. One position per symbol.

Exits are prices, not dates: the target and the stop. History says those resolve in a median of 6 trading days, a quarter inside 3 and three quarters inside 10.

Optionally through Alpaca

The built-in book fills instantly at the quoted price with no fees and no queue, which makes it an optimistic bound. Alpaca's paper environment is more honest — real market hours, real order handling, orders that sit unfilled when the market is shut. Add a free paper key to a git-ignored .env and SONAR uses it:

APCA_API_KEY_ID=PK...        # paper keys start with PK
APCA_API_SECRET_KEY=...
On the guards. Alpaca's live and paper APIs differ by one hostname. The host is a module constant with no parameter to override; a key that is not clearly a paper key is refused before any request; the account is checked on connect; and every request re-checks all of it. Each failure raises. Going live is not a setting in this app — it is a decision for a human with an account, and SONAR does not implement it.

7Testing it yourself

The Lab tab exists so none of this has to be taken on trust. Everything in it runs on real historical bars through the same replay the research used.

Simulate

Pick a universe, a range, a horizon and a sampling step, and the model is replayed over every decision point in that window. The result reports the realised hit rate beside what the barrier maths predicted, with the two-standard-error band — because a hit rate on its own means nothing. When the difference sits inside its error bar the tab says so plainly: that is the result, not a failure to find one.

Attribute

A hit rate tells you whether the model worked. It cannot tell you which part of it did, and that is the only version of the question you can act on. Each component of the confidence score is asked the question its weight is a claim about — does ranking on this sort winners from losers? — three ways:

ReadingWhat it answers
ICRank correlation between the component and the outcome.
Quintile spreadHit rate in the top fifth minus the bottom fifth, with the error bar on that difference.
Leave-one-outThe blend's IC without this component. If dropping it does not lower the blend, the weight is buying nothing.

The verdicts are KEEP, WEAK, DROP and INVERTED. The last one matters most: a component with a significant negative IC is not merely useless — carried with a positive weight it actively pushes the wrong instruments up the board. p-values go through Benjamini-Hochberg together, because testing four components and reporting the best one is how noise gets published.

The catalyst component reports not measured rather than passing: the replay has no historical earnings calendar, and an untested component must not read as a validated one.

Replay

The simulation grades the model. Replay grades you. The app's whole position on the asset board is that direction is your call, and this is where you find out whether those calls are any good.

You get one setup at a time on real history — price, momentum, volatility, the score, the target and the stop — and you buy, short or skip. Everything after the cursor is withheld: the chart stops where you are, and the later bars are not in the object the screen was handed, so there is nothing to peek at even by accident. There is no going back, because retaking a decision whose outcome you have seen is the same problem wearing a different hat.

The model's call on the same setup is scored alongside yours whether or not you skip, so skipping the hard ones cannot look like skill. Each call risks a fixed amount and a win pays the reward-to-risk multiple — risk-sized rather than notional, so being wrong about a volatile coin costs exactly what being wrong about a quiet currency pair costs. Otherwise the P&L would be measuring volatility instead of judgement.

The scorecard reports your hit rate with its error bar next to the model's and the barrier baseline. Below twenty resolved calls it refuses to draw a conclusion, which is the same rule the app applies to itself.

8Alerts & where you could trade

Alerts say what changed

An alert is a delivery mechanism for the confidence score, and that score is a notability heuristic — so that is all an alert says. It never says buy, it never says short, and there is a test asserting those words cannot appear in one.

That is the evidence rather than caution. Five studies found no directional edge, and the attribution above measured the blended score's IC as negative — so an alert firing on a high score and shouting BUY would be pointing at the wrong instruments with an air of authority. The honest route to a directional alert runs through the calibration table: once the paper book has a measured edge, gating on that is earned.

Alerts fire on a transition, not a level. "News went Normal → Spike" happens once; "news is Spike" would re-fire every ninety seconds until the story aged out, which teaches you to ignore the feature. Rises only, one hour of cooldown per instrument and kind, and the first scan after a restart sets the baseline silently.

Every alert carries the age of the data it fired on. An equity quote outside market hours can be six hours old, and an alert that does not admit that is claiming an immediacy it does not have.

Where you could actually trade it

The board scores an index, two futures contracts and a delisted coin next to ordinary shares. Nine of twenty-six rows cannot be bought as shown, so every row carries a marker and a tooltip saying what you would actually buy and where — or that you could not.

MarkerMeaning
↗Tradeable through something else — an index through a UCITS ETF, a futures contract through an ETC.
⊘Not available on any mainstream regulated venue.

Three specifics worth knowing. US-domiciled ETFs — SPY, VOO, QQQ — are closed to EU retail entirely under PRIIPs, because they publish no Key Information Document; the UCITS versions are the route. Monero was delisted by Binance globally in February 2024 and by Kraken across the whole EEA in October 2024, so it is legal to own and not buyable here. And converting EUR to USD in a banking app is not holding an FX position, though the apps that do the first are routinely assumed to do the second.

This is reference information, not advice, and it goes stale — the tooltip carries the date it was last verified.

9How the score gets graded

Every closed position carries the confidence it was opened on, so the Book tab can ask the only question that matters: did high scores actually win more often? Hit rate is bucketed by score and compared with what the barrier maths advertised.

Below 20 closed positions it reports nothing at all. A five-win streak is not an edge, and a young install showing an empty table is the honest state rather than a bug.

The run backtest button answers the same question in seconds instead of months, by replaying the identical plan over years of real bars — momentum and volatility from prior bars only, then walking forward through actual highs and lows. A bar that spans both barriers scores as a loss, since daily data cannot order them, and costs are excluded. Reality is worse than what it reports, not better.

10What the research found

Five studies, each more careful than the last. All of them negative.

QuestionAnswer
Does momentum predict the barrier outcome? No — flat across buckets, and worse than baseline for the largest moves.
Does a news/attention spike? No — +0.8 points against a ±3.1 error bar, over 25,504 setups.
Does anything sort the cross-section? No — 0 of 16 features survived false-discovery correction over 116,563 rows.
Did the one surviving lead replicate? No — it lived in a single period, appeared in crypto rather than the equities its theory requires, and had no orderly decay.
Does anything work conditionally? No — 0 of 48 feature-by-regime tests, with the floor set by a control.
The most useful thing the research produced was a warning. In one holdout window a seeded random number — a feature that cannot predict anything — reached t = −1.87, while real features sat at +2.4 to +3.4 and looked like discoveries. Daily cross-sections are correlated, so a single window holds far fewer independent observations than its date count suggests. Candidates are now scored against the noise floor measured in their own window, never a textbook threshold.

An earlier version of these docs claimed a news spike was worth +4.9 points. It did not survive a larger sample and fell to +0.8. That claim is recorded as withdrawn rather than quietly deleted, because silently editing it would leave exactly the false impression this project exists to argue against.

Two things are still untested, not cleared: SONAR's own word-list sentiment (Wikipedia pageviews measure attention volume, not tone), and anything requiring intraday or order-flow data.

11Running it & what it costs

A native macOS app — PySide6 widgets, every chart drawn with QPainter, no web view.

uv venv .venv && uv pip install -r requirements.txt
python main.py              # the app
python main.py --selftest   # check a build's wiring and exit
python main.py --headless   # the HTTP daemon instead

Does it cost money?

No. It runs locally, every source is a free public API with no key and no billing, it places no real trades, and the running app makes zero AI/LLM calls — the model is local arithmetic and the news sentiment is a local word list. Building SONAR used AI; running it does not.

The only resource is bandwidth, roughly 8MB an hour since the Polymarket board was removed — down from about 60MB. Negligible on broadband, and worth knowing on a metered connection.

Closing it

The close button quits. It used to hide to the menu bar and leave the engine running — the equity curve only means something if positions settle on the hours they were priced for — but on macOS the red button closes, and an app that survives it reads as one that ignored you.

If you want the engine running without the window, that is what the daemon is for: python main.py --headless, or the launchd agent in scripts/install_agent.sh. The menu-bar item is a readout and a second Quit, not the only way back in.

12Honesty & limits

This is not financial advice and it places no real trades. Paper P/L predicts nothing about real results. Live trading means real fees, real spread, real slippage and real risk — none of which a simulation pays.
  • No edge has been found. Five studies, progressively more careful, all negative. That is the expected answer for liquid instruments priced by people running the same arithmetic.
  • Confidence is notability, not the probability of profit. P(PROF) is that, and it sits at its driftless baseline because no drift has been measured.
  • Small samples lie. Dozens of positions cannot separate skill from luck. An early run showed +114% on a 44% win rate, carried entirely by three longshot wins — variance wearing a costume.
  • News is context, never instruction. Scraped text is treated as untrusted data: read and summarised, never acted upon.
  • Fills are optimistic. The internal book ignores spread, slippage and fees; the backtest excludes costs and resolves ambiguous bars against you.

By design SONAR stops one step short of acting. It will take you up to a decision with its reasoning fully exposed, and leave the decision to you.

13Playmaker — pricing a sports bet

The same discipline as the rest of the app, pointed at sport. It prices a wager rather than predicting one, and it will not let a language model size a bet for you.

Seven sports, in three shapes

NFL, College Football, NBA, MMA (UFC), International Football, Golf and Cycling — 67 prop types between them, named here exactly as the dropdown names them. Football means international team games only: World Cup, Euros, Copa América, Gold Cup, AFCON, Nations League, every confederation's qualifiers and friendlies. No club competitions.

The pricing half of the tab works identically for all seven — it is odds arithmetic and never learns what it is pricing. The rating half does not, because a result does not mean the same thing in each:

ShapeSportsWhat can be fitted
Two sides, a scorelineNFL, College Football, NBA, International Football Elo with margin of victory. Football also gets Dixon-Coles, which produces a whole distribution over scorelines.
Two sides, no scorelineMMA Elo on wins and losses. There is no margin to learn from, so the model correctly degenerates to plain Elo.
A fieldGolf, Cycling Nothing. A hundred-odd competitors and a finishing order is not a contest between two sides. Pretending Elo applies would be inventing an answer rather than admitting there isn't one.
Cycling has no results feed at all. ESPN serves no cycling endpoint, and the free alternatives are unofficial scrapers of a single site. So cycling is priced and screened like any other sport — that arithmetic needs no data — and nothing here rates a rider. The tab says so when you select it, rather than showing you an empty panel and letting you wonder.

International football pools fourteen competitions into one set of national-team ratings. National sides play a handful of times a year, so splitting them by tournament would leave no single competition with enough matches to fit on — and a side's form in a qualifier is evidence about it at the Euros.

Getting the bookmaker's cut out

A price implies a probability, but it implies a worse one than the bookmaker really believes, because their margin is baked in (see §1). Removing it is called devigging, and there are three defensible ways to do it that give different answers:

MethodHow it splits the margin
MultiplicativeEvenly, in proportion to each price. The one every online calculator uses — and the one comparative studies rank last on every measure.
PowerClarke's method: find the exponent that makes the probabilities sum to 1. Best or equal-best in the same comparison.
ShinTreats the margin as the bookmaker's protection against people who know something. Generally the best calibrated, and what Playmaker uses by default.

This is not hair-splitting. On a −2000 / +1100 market the proportional method calls the longshot 8.05% and Shin calls it 6.55% — 23% apart. Run it the other way and the proportional method invents an edge on the favourite that is not there. The tab prints all three side by side so you can see the disagreement rather than take it on trust.

One price tells you nothing. A margin is defined as the amount by which a market's prices sum past certainty, so you cannot see it from one side. −110 on its own is equally consistent with a coin flip carrying normal juice and with a genuine 52.4% favourite quoted at no margin at all. Playmaker asks for every side of the market because the arithmetic requires it.

The cross-book screen

The one approach here with a published track record, and it does not forecast anything. Kaunitz, Zhong and Kreiner (2017) took the odds many bookmakers were already publishing, formed a consensus, and bet only where one book was a clear outlier against it. Profitable over a ten-year simulation and five months of real staked money.

Paste three or more books into the tab and it runs exactly that. Each row's consensus excludes that row's own book, so a price cannot vote for itself. It says nothing about who wins — only that one bookmaker disagrees with the rest.

The same paper's other finding. The bookmakers limited the accounts that kept winning. The edge is real and the capacity for it is very small. Anyone who quotes the first half of that result without the second is selling you something.

The models, and what they scored

Three, all chosen because they have genuine track records rather than because they are clever:

ModelWhat it does
EloA rating per team that moves after every game, scaled by the margin and corrected so that strong teams' ratings do not run away. The reviewed literature says a tuned Elo captures most of what any pre-match model can.
Dixon-ColesFor football: an attack and a defence strength per team producing a full distribution over scorelines, so the draw, the total and both-teams-to-score all fall out of one fit. Older matches count for less, on a half-life of about a year.
PythagoreanA cross-check: expected win rate from goals or points scored and conceded, which predicts a team's future record better than its current one does.

MMA barely works, and the app says so. Across 3,369 fights Elo beat the base rate by three thousandths — the ratings themselves look sensible (the fighters at the top are the ones you would name), but they predict almost nothing a coin could not. That is not a surprise: fighters compete twice a year, styles matter more than general strength, and one punch ends it. The verdict is WEAK rather than KEEP, which doubles the uncertainty band on any MMA estimate and shrinks the stake accordingly. A model that cannot tell you much should not be allowed to sound like it can.

Dixon-Coles, fitted separately on club-league matches to check the implementation: home advantage 1.155 goals-multiplier, ranked probability score 0.2149 on held-out matches against a published bar around 0.20.

Beating the base rate is a real result and a modest one. It is not the same as beating a bookmaker, who starts from a better price than the base rate and charges you the margin in §1 on top.

What cannot size a bet

The tab also asks a language model to read the matchup. Its opinion is shown and is explicitly not used for the edge, the expected value or the stake. It used to be: a percentage scraped out of prose with a pattern match, turned into a Kelly stake. Correct arithmetic wrapped around an unmeasured number reads as far more credible than either half deserves, which is the most dangerous shape a tool like this can take.

Every probability now carries where it came from. A model's may size a bet only after it has been measured out of sample, and its interval is set by its measured calibration error rather than by anything it claims about itself. Stakes are sized at the pessimistic end of that interval, so an honest uncertainty that spans break-even produces no bet at all — which is the answer a single number can never give.
