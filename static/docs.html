SONAR — how it works

An honest market scanner: live prices, live prediction-market odds, reputable news, and a probability model — with paper money instead of promises. It began as a recreation of the viral "an AI built an overnight trading bot" dashboard, with the marketing stripped out.

The one thing to understand first. SONAR reads real market data and real news, but it never touches real money. There is no exchange account, no wallet, no broker connection, and no order is ever placed anywhere. It exists to show — transparently — how a model and a set of heuristics stack up against live markets, and how thin and noisy any "edge" really is.

1What it is

SONAR has three views, sharing one data layer:

ViewWhat it doesDoes it assert a side?
Terminal The original BTC hourly up/down paper-trading dashboard. A probability model prices each hour, compares itself to Polymarket, and takes at most one simulated bet per hour. Yes — a real (paper) edge, because the model prices the market independently.
Scanner Ranks many live Polymarket markets — crypto, economy, politics, geopolitics — by a transparent confidence heuristic, with matched news. Only for crypto up/down markets. Elsewhere: the market's own odds, no call.
Assets A screener over real instruments — equities, indices, forex, crypto spot, commodities — with price, momentum, volatility and matched news. No — a computed "lean" indicator only. There is no independent model here.

The core loop, everywhere: read reality → form a view → compare to the crowd → (on the terminal) act on paper. Run it long enough and the equity curve answers one question: does the model actually beat the market, or is it riding luck? (Spoiler: mostly luck.)

3The model — a barrier probability

Part-way through the hour we already know the open o and the current price c. What's uncertain is the move over the time that's left. The model treats the rest of the hour as a driftless random walk (Bitcoin's hourly return is, to a good approximation, a coin flip around zero). With σ the per-hour volatility and τ the fraction of the hour remaining:

P(up) = Φ( ln(c / o) / (σ · √τ) )

where Φ is the standard normal CDF. In plain words: how many standard deviations of the remaining move is the current price above the open? The further above, and the less time left, the more certain an Up close.

  • At the top of the hour (τ = 1, c = o) it returns exactly 0.5 — no information, no edge.
  • As the hour runs out (τ → 0) it collapses toward 1 or 0 depending only on whether price is currently above or below the open.

The model's only disagreement with the market comes from the volatility estimate: it uses realised vol (recent hourly moves) while the market prices its own implied vol. When they differ, there's a thin statistical edge — the realised-vs-implied trade that real quants run. It is small, and frequently negative once you pay the spread.

4Paper trading — sizing & settlement

The engine holds at most one position per hour. It enters only when both are true:

  • the absolute edge |model − market| exceeds 4¢, and
  • there's a sensible amount of time left (roughly the middle ~70% of the hour — not the noisy first minutes, not the final seconds).

Stake is a capped, half-Kelly fraction of the bankroll — bigger when the edge is bigger, but never more than 8% on one hour. Entry crosses the spread (you pay the ask), so the fill price is realistic. Buying the Up share at price p means each share costs p and pays 1.00 if the hour closes up, 0.00 if not.

At the top of the next hour the position is settled on the real candle: a winning share is worth 1.00, a loser 0.00, and the realised profit or loss updates the bankroll and the equity curve.

Why a below-50% win rate can still show a profit. The model often backs cheap longshots (say 25¢). Those lose most of the time, but each win pays ~4× the stake. A handful of those wins can lift the total even when fewer than half the bets hit — which is exactly why a rising paper balance is not proof of edge. It's usually variance. This is not hypothetical: an earlier run showed +114% on a 44% win rate, carried by three longshot hits.

5The backtest seed

A brand-new run would have an empty chart, so on first launch the equity curve is seeded with a fair-odds backtest over the last 36 real hours: the model's favoured side, priced at its own fair probability, settled on the real candle. Because the odds are fair, the expected value is ≈ 0 by construction — it illustrates variance, not profit, and invents no counterparty. On the chart these seed hours are the grey segment before the gold LIVE line; in the trade log they carry a small bt tag.

7The market scanner

The Scanner widens the tool from one BTC market to many live Polymarket markets across crypto, economics, politics and geopolitics (sports are excluded). Each market gets a confidence score (0–100) — and this is the part to read honestly:

Confidence is not the probability you'll profit. It's a transparent heuristic for how notable and tradeable a setup looks right now, blended from measurable factors. Every card shows the mix as a bar, so it's never a black box.
ComponentMeaningWeight (crypto / other)
model edgeModel probability minus market price — the only true edge signal0.30 / —
liquidity24h volume and book depth0.20 / 0.32
timingHow soon it resolves (sooner scores higher — the "quick" view)0.15 / 0.26
momentumHow far the odds have moved recently0.15 / 0.22
newsRecency-weighted matched headlines0.20 / 0.20

A side (Up/Down, on paper) is asserted only for crypto up/down markets, where the barrier model gives an independent probability. For every other market there's no independent model, so the scanner shows the market's own odds and the news around it — no buy/sell call.

News as context

Headlines come from the eight reputable public feeds in §2, keyword-matched to each market and shown with source and link. Matching is deliberately conservative — months, weekdays and stopwords are stripped, and a match needs a distinctive shared term — so a shared "July" can't fake a connection. Two rules: it's context, not a predictor (sentiment is a crude word-list heuristic, labelled as such), and scraped text is treated as untrusted data — the tool reads and summarises it, never acts on anything written inside a headline or article.

8The assets screener

The $ Assets tab on the Scanner covers 17 real instruments: 7 equities (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, TSLA, AMZN, GOOGL, META), 3 indices (S&P 500, Nasdaq, Dow), 3 FX pairs (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY), 2 crypto spot (BTC, ETH) and 2 commodities (gold, WTI crude). Each card shows live price, 1-day and 5-day change, daily volatility, a sparkline, matched news, and a confidence score.

There is no model here — and that matters. Unlike a prediction market, a stock has no "fair value" SONAR can compute independently. So the Bullish / Bearish / Neutral lean is nothing more than the sign of (5-day momentum + crude news sentiment). It is a computed technical indicator, shown transparently. It is not a recommendation, and it is not advice.

Asset confidence is weighted news 0.45 · momentum 0.35 · volatility 0.20 — deliberately news-heavy, because for a real asset "what's happening right now" is the honest thing to surface, and a price move alone tells you little about why.

9Why BTC drives the model

SONAR now covers many markets and many assets, but the barrier model — the only component that produces a genuine edge — still runs on crypto up/down markets. That's a matter of what can be priced honestly, not a limitation of the code:

  • The model needs an open, a current price, a volatility and a clock. Crypto hourly markets supply all four, exactly and for free.
  • The resolution source (a single Binance 1-hour candle) is unambiguous — no judgement calls, no disputed outcomes.
  • Polymarket's most liquid cleanly-resolving hourly series is BTC's; ETH has an equivalent one and SONAR prices it the same way. Most other assets have no such market.

For an election, a Fed decision or a stock, there is no comparable independent pricing — so SONAR deliberately declines to invent one. That silence is the honest answer, not a missing feature.

10Running it & what it costs

Zero dependencies — Python 3.11+ standard library only:

python3 -m sonar.server        # then open http://127.0.0.1:8787
# options:  --host 0.0.0.0  --port 9000

Leave it running and the equity curve gains a point each hour as markets resolve. State persists to data/state.json (delete that file to reset to a clean $10,000).

Does it cost money?

No. It runs locally on your machine; every data source is a free public API with no key and no billing; it places no trades, so there are no fees; and — worth stating plainly — the running app makes zero AI/LLM calls. The "model" is local arithmetic and the news sentiment is a local word list. Building SONAR used AI; running it does not.

The only real resource is bandwidth, measured live:

ComponentCadence≈ per hour

Hourly market + order bookevery 15 s~3.5 MB
News (8 feeds)cached 8 min~2.2 MB

Total~60 MB/hour ≈ 1.4 GB/day if left running 24/7

Negligible on home broadband; worth knowing on a metered or mobile connection. It's dominated by the market scan — widening that interval to 5 minutes cuts total traffic by roughly 70%. Free APIs may throttle if hammered, but they never bill.

13Honesty & limits

This is not financial advice, and it places no real trades. Paper P/L predicts nothing about real results. Live trading would mean real fees, real spread, real slippage, and real risk — none of which a simulation pays.
  • The edge is thin and often negative. Crossing the real bid/ask spread eats most of any realised-vs-implied advantage.
  • Small samples lie. Dozens of hours is not enough to distinguish skill from luck; a cheerful return number is usually the latter. See the +114% / 44%-win-rate example in §4.
  • Confidence scores are heuristics, not probabilities of profit — and the asset "lean" is momentum plus a word-list, nothing more.
  • News is context, never instruction. Sentiment from a small lexicon does not forecast prices, and scraped text is never treated as a command.
  • Settlement is approximated at the hour boundary using the contiguous candle price (accurate to within a few dollars), not a separate authoritative fetch.
  • The market is efficient. These hourly crypto markets are priced by people doing the same math; persistent free money is not on the table.

By design, SONAR stops one step short of acting: it will take you right up to a decision, with its reasoning fully exposed, and leave the decision to you.
